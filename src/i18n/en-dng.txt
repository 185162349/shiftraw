PROSE_H2: Why convert DNG to 
PROSE0: DNG (Digital Negative) is Adobe's open RAW format, published in 2004 as a standardized alternative to the zoo of proprietary camera formats. Some cameras write DNG natively — Leica, Pentax, Ricoh, DJI drones, most Blackmagic cinema cameras — and Lightroom can convert any proprietary RAW into DNG for archival.
PROSE1: Being open does not make DNG files any easier to share: they are still raw sensor data, still large, and still ignored by anything that isn't photo software. If you archived your library as DNG and now need web-ready images, a converter is the bridge.
PROSE2: ShiftRaw converts DNG to JPG in your browser with no upload. The LibRaw engine handles both linear and mosaic DNG variants, and your files never leave your device.
PROSE3: <strong>Best for:</strong> JPG is the universal choice: every device, browser, app and platform displays it, and at 90%+ quality the compression is invisible. A 45-megapixel RAW frame converts to a 5–10 MB JPG that uploads anywhere. For web and client delivery it remains the sensible default — start here unless you specifically need transparency (PNG) or the smallest possible files (WebP).

FAQ0Q: Is DNG better than proprietary RAW formats like CR2 or NEF?
FAQ0A: DNG is better for archiving and portability: the specification is open, so software will likely be able to read it decades from now. Proprietary formats sometimes carry slightly more camera-specific data. Many photographers shoot proprietary RAW and archive to DNG — ShiftRaw converts either kind.
FAQ1Q: Will converting DNG to JPG preserve my Lightroom edits?
FAQ1A: Only if you exported the DNG from Lightroom with edits baked in, or exported a linear DNG. A plain raw DNG carries the original sensor data; ShiftRaw renders it with the embedded as-shot white balance.
FAQ2Q: Why is my DNG file so big?
FAQ2A: If the DNG is linear (fully demosaiced) or contains the original RAW embedded — an option in Lightroom's conversion settings — the file can be larger than the source RAW. For delivery purposes, converting to JPG typically reduces file size by 85–95%.
FAQ3Q: Can I convert DNG back to CR2 or NEF?
FAQ3A: No — proprietary camera formats are not openly writable, and DNG conversion is one-way. That's exactly why DNG exists as the archival endpoint, and why converting DNG to universally readable formats like JPG or PNG is the practical path for sharing.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
