PROSE0: WebP is Google's image format, introduced in 2010 and now the default for much of the web — Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all display it, and tools like Squoosh, cwebp and most CDN pipelines export it. WebP files are typically 25–35% smaller than a comparable JPEG, which is why it spread so fast.

For web and client delivery it remains the sensible default — start here unless you specifically need transparency (PNG) or the smallest possible files (WebP).

ABOUT_H2: About the WebP format
CARD0 [Developer]: Google
CARD1 [Introduced]: September 2010
CARD2 [Compression]: VP8 (lossy) or VP8L (lossless) intra-frame
CARD3 [Size advantage]: 25–35% smaller than JPEG at equal quality
CARD4 [Extras]: Alpha transparency and animation support
CARD5 [Browser support]: Universal since ~2020
CARD6 [Weak spot]: Older desktop apps and some print workflows
FAQ0Q: Why can't I open WebP files in Photoshop or Word?
FAQ0A: WebP support arrived in desktop software late — Photoshop only gained it in version 23.2 (2022), and many Office and viewer installs still lack the codec. Converting to JPG or PNG sidesteps the issue entirely.
FAQ1Q: Will converting WebP to JPG make files bigger?
FAQ1A: Yes, typically 25–40% bigger at similar visual quality — that's the efficiency you're trading away for compatibility. It's usually the right trade when a person or platform on the other end simply needs a standard file.
FAQ2Q: Is WebP lossy or lossless?
FAQ2A: Both — the format supports a lossy VP8 mode (the common case, comparable to JPEG) and a lossless VP8L mode. ShiftRaw handles either; the source encoding just affects how much additional loss a JPG conversion introduces.
FAQ3Q: Does converting WebP to PNG lose quality?
FAQ3A: No. PNG is lossless, so the pixels you get are exactly the pixels in the WebP file. The file will be larger — often several times larger — because PNG's compression is far less aggressive than WebP's.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
