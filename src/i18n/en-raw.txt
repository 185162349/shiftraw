FAQ0Q: Which RAW formats does this converter support?
FAQ0A: All the common ones: Canon CR2/CR3, Nikon NEF, Sony ARW, Adobe DNG, Fujifilm RAF, Panasonic RW2, Olympus ORF, Pentax PEF, Leica DNG, plus a long tail of exotic formats (3FR, FFF, IIQ, MRW, X3F and more). If it's a RAW file from a mainstream camera made in the last two decades, it will very likely decode.
FAQ1Q: Why shoot RAW instead of JPG?
FAQ1A: RAW keeps 12–14 bits of data per channel against JPG's 8, and defers every processing decision — white balance, exposure, profile — until editing. Overexposed skies and muddy shadows that are unrecoverable in JPG can often be rescued from RAW. The trade-off is exactly what this tool solves: RAW for capture and editing, instant conversion for delivery.
FAQ2Q: Does converting RAW to JPG reduce image quality?
FAQ2A: You lose bit depth and gain lossy compression, but at the default 92% quality the result is visually identical for screen use and normal print sizes. Serious editors keep the RAW originals; everyone converts copies for sharing.
FAQ3Q: Is my work safe here?
FAQ3A: Yes — this is a client-side converter. The RAW decoding engine is downloaded to your browser as WebAssembly and runs on your CPU. Your files are never transmitted, stored or seen by any server. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it will still work.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
