PROSE0: ARW (Alpha RAW) is Sony's RAW format, written by every camera in the Alpha line — the a7 and a9 full-frame mirrorless bodies that dominate the market, the APS-C a6000 series, and older Alpha DSLRs and SLTs. FAQ0Q: What does ARW stand for?
FAQ0A: Alpha Raw — Sony's RAW image format for the Alpha camera system. You may also encounter SRF and SR2 files from earlier Sony DSLRs; those are supported here too.
FAQ1Q: Why are my ARW files 80 MB or larger?
FAQ1A: High-resolution bodies like the a7R V store 61 megapixels at 14-bit depth, and if your camera is set to Uncompressed RAW the files balloon to 120+ MB. Switching the camera to Lossless Compressed cuts size roughly in half with no quality loss; converting to JPG shrinks delivery files by 90%.
FAQ2Q: Can I convert ARW to JPG on any computer?
FAQ2A: Yes — that's the point of ShiftRaw. The decoder is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari). No Sony software, no Capture One, no installation.
FAQ3Q: Will the conversion keep my Sony colors?
FAQ3A: The converter applies the white balance your camera recorded at capture time and outputs standard sRGB. Because Sony's JPEG engine adds its own creative styling on top of raw data, expect a clean, neutral rendering rather than an in-camera Creative Look — ideal as a base for further grading.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
