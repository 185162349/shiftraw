PROSE0: HEIC (High Efficiency Image Container) is the format every iPhone has used by default since iOS 11 in 2017. It wraps HEVC-compressed image data — the same codec family as modern video — and delivers photos at roughly half the file size of a same-quality JPEG. Apple adopted it to save storage; the rest of the world hasn't fully caught up.

FAQ0Q: Why do my iPhone photos end up as HEIC?
FAQ0A: Since iOS 11, Apple saves camera photos as HEIC by default to halve storage use. Settings › Camera › Formats has a 'Most Compatible' option that switches back to JPG — but converting here is easier than re-shooting and keeps the smaller originals.
FAQ1Q: Will converting HEIC to JPG make the file bigger?
FAQ1A: Usually yes, somewhat — HEIC is the more efficient codec, so a maximum-quality JPG export lands at 1.5–2.5× the HEIC size. In exchange you get a file that opens instantly on every device, platform and browser made in the last 30 years.
FAQ2Q: Does HEIC to JPG lose quality?
FAQ2A: Both formats are lossy, so there is a generational loss, but at 92% quality it is visually imperceptible. For a pixel-perfect copy choose PNG instead, which is lossless (and correspondingly larger).
FAQ3Q: Do Live Photos survive the conversion?
FAQ3A: No — a Live Photo is a HEIC still plus a short video file, and converting extracts only the still image. That's true of every HEIC-to-JPG converter; the video component needs a separate export.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
