PROSE_H2: Why convert RAF to 
PROSE0: RAF is Fujifilm's RAW format, and Fujifilm is the interesting outlier in the RAW world: since 2012 its X-series cameras use X-Trans sensors with a 6×6 color filter array that deliberately avoids the Bayer pattern, trading demosaicing simplicity for reduced moiré and stronger color fidelity.
PROSE1: That unusual sensor design is why RAF files need specific processing — generic converters that assume Bayer data render X-Trans files soft or artifacted. ShiftRaw's LibRaw engine includes proper X-Trans interpolation, so your files get the full-resolution treatment they deserve.
PROSE2: Converting RAF to JPG happens entirely in your browser: the decoder is WebAssembly, the camera's white balance is applied, and your photos never touch a server.
PROSE3: <strong>Best for:</strong> JPG is the universal choice: every device, browser, app and platform displays it, and at 90%+ quality the compression is invisible. A 45-megapixel RAW frame converts to a 5–10 MB JPG that uploads anywhere. For web and client delivery it remains the sensible default — start here unless you specifically need transparency (PNG) or the smallest possible files (WebP).

FAQ0Q: Why do RAF files look different in different apps?
FAQ0A: X-Trans sensors need dedicated demosaicing, and every renderer interprets them slightly differently. Fujifilm's own apps apply film simulations on top of raw data; ShiftRaw renders a clean, neutral interpretation with as-shot white balance — a solid base for your own grading.
FAQ1Q: Can this converter handle GFX medium-format RAF files?
FAQ1A: Yes. GFX 50S/100S RAF files are large (100 MP from the GFX100 II) but supported — decoding just takes a few seconds longer. Memory-wise, the browser handles up to roughly 100 MP comfortably on a modern machine.
FAQ2Q: What are film simulations and do they survive conversion?
FAQ2A: Film simulations like Classic Chrome or Acros are rendering profiles applied when Fujifilm software develops the RAF. The raw file stores only the tag of the selected simulation. ShiftRaw outputs a neutral rendering; for the simulation look, shoot JPG+RAW or use Fujifilm's app.
FAQ3Q: Why are RAF files bigger than same-megapixel Canon or Sony files?
FAQ3A: Fujifilm stores the raw mosaic at 14-bit with lossless compression, and X-Trans's irregular pattern compresses less efficiently than Bayer. A 26 MP X-T4 RAF runs ~50 MB where a 24 MP Bayer RAW might be 25–35 MB.
FAQ4Q: JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
FAQ4A: JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
FAQ5Q: Is there a file size or batch limit?
FAQ5A: No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
