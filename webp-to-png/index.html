Why convert WebP to

WebP is Google's image format, introduced in 2010 and now the default for much of the web — Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all display it, and tools like Squoosh, cwebp and most CDN pipelines export it. WebP files are typically 25–35% smaller than a comparable JPEG, which is why it spread so fast.

The friction runs the other way: a pile of WebP downloads from the web won't open in older desktop software — Photoshop before 23.2, many Microsoft Office versions, older image viewers — and some upload forms, print shops and client workflows still demand a JPG or PNG.

ShiftRaw converts WebP to PNG entirely in your browser. Modern browsers decode WebP natively, so conversion is instant, pixel-accurate and completely private.

Best for: PNG is lossless — every pixel of the converted image is preserved exactly, with no compression artifacts, and it supports full alpha transparency. The trade-off is size: expect files 3–10× larger than an equivalent JPG. Choose PNG when quality is non-negotiable, when you need transparency, or when the image will be edited further before final delivery.

Frequently asked questions

Why can't I open WebP files in Photoshop or Word?
WebP support arrived in desktop software late — Photoshop only gained it in version 23.2 (2022), and many Office and viewer installs still lack the codec. Converting to JPG or PNG sidesteps the issue entirely.
Will converting WebP to JPG make files bigger?
Yes, typically 25–40% bigger at similar visual quality — that's the efficiency you're trading away for compatibility. It's usually the right trade when a person or platform on the other end simply needs a standard file.
Is WebP lossy or lossless?
Both — the format supports a lossy VP8 mode (the common case, comparable to JPEG) and a lossless VP8L mode. ShiftRaw handles either; the source encoding just affects how much additional loss a JPG conversion introduces.
Does converting WebP to PNG lose quality?
No. PNG is lossless, so the pixels you get are exactly the pixels in the WebP file. The file will be larger — often several times larger — because PNG's compression is far less aggressive than WebP's.
When is PNG worth the larger file size?
When you need a pixel-perfect lossless copy, transparency, or plan to re-edit and re-save the file — PNG survives round-trips without quality loss. For straightforward photo sharing, JPG at high quality looks identical at a fraction of the size.
Is there a file size or batch limit?
No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
