Why convert ARW to

ARW (Alpha RAW) is Sony's RAW format, written by every camera in the Alpha line — the a7 and a9 full-frame mirrorless bodies that dominate the market, the APS-C a6000 series, and older Alpha DSLRs and SLTs. An ARW file contains 12- or 14-bit sensor data plus Sony's rich metadata, in lossy-compressed, lossless-compressed or uncompressed variants depending on your camera menu settings.

Sony's file naming (DSC01234.ARW) makes batches easy to accumulate and hard to deliver: clients, galleries and social platforms universally expect JPG, PNG or WebP.

ShiftRaw converts ARW to WebP entirely in your browser. A WebAssembly build of LibRaw handles the decoding with your camera's white balance applied, so your photos stay on your machine from start to finish.

Best for: WebP is the modern option: 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, with alpha-channel support, and every current browser displays it. If your images are headed for the web — a site, blog, or web app — WebP is the best-performing choice and has been natively supported across all major browsers since 2020.

Frequently asked questions

What does ARW stand for?
Alpha Raw — Sony's RAW image format for the Alpha camera system. You may also encounter SRF and SR2 files from earlier Sony DSLRs; those are supported here too.
Why are my ARW files 80 MB or larger?
High-resolution bodies like the a7R V store 61 megapixels at 14-bit depth, and if your camera is set to Uncompressed RAW the files balloon to 120+ MB. Switching the camera to Lossless Compressed cuts size roughly in half with no quality loss; converting to JPG shrinks delivery files by 90%.
Can I convert ARW to JPG on any computer?
Yes — that's the point of ShiftRaw. The decoder is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in any modern browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari). No Sony software, no Capture One, no installation.
Will the conversion keep my Sony colors?
The converter applies the white balance your camera recorded at capture time and outputs standard sRGB. Because Sony's JPEG engine adds its own creative styling on top of raw data, expect a clean, neutral rendering rather than an in-camera Creative Look — ideal as a base for further grading.
Is WebP safe to use everywhere now?
On the web, yes — Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all support it natively, and it now accounts for a large share of served images. Desktop software is the weak spot: older Photoshop, Office and viewer versions may not open it. For web use, choose WebP; for files that must open anywhere, choose JPG.
Is there a file size or batch limit?
No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
