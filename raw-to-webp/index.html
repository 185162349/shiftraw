Why convert RAW to WebP?

Every RAW file — whatever its three-letter extension — is the same idea: the unprocessed data read straight off your camera's sensor, before demosaicing, white balance, sharpening or compression. Camera makers each wrap that data in their own container (CR2 and CR3 from Canon, NEF from Nikon, ARW from Sony, RAF from Fujifilm, DNG from Adobe and others), which is why RAW is powerful for editing and painful for sharing.

This converter speaks all of them. Drop in files from any major manufacturer — Canon, Nikon, Sony, Fujifilm, Panasonic, Olympus/OM System, Pentax, Leica, Hasselblad or DJI — and the LibRaw engine identifies and decodes each one automatically, applying the camera's recorded white balance.

Converting RAW to WebP runs 100% in your browser via WebAssembly. Your photos never leave your device, there's no file-size quota, no watermark and no sign-up.

Best for: WebP is the modern option: 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, with alpha-channel support, and every current browser displays it. If your images are headed for the web — a site, blog, or web app — WebP is the best-performing choice and has been natively supported across all major browsers since 2020.

Frequently asked questions

Which RAW formats does this converter support?
All the common ones: Canon CR2/CR3, Nikon NEF, Sony ARW, Adobe DNG, Fujifilm RAF, Panasonic RW2, Olympus ORF, Pentax PEF, Leica DNG, plus a long tail of exotic formats (3FR, FFF, IIQ, MRW, X3F and more). If it's a RAW file from a mainstream camera made in the last two decades, it will very likely decode.
Why shoot RAW instead of JPG?
RAW keeps 12–14 bits of data per channel against JPG's 8, and defers every processing decision — white balance, exposure, profile — until editing. Overexposed skies and muddy shadows that are unrecoverable in JPG can often be rescued from RAW. The trade-off is exactly what this tool solves: RAW for capture and editing, instant conversion for delivery.
Does converting RAW to JPG reduce image quality?
You lose bit depth and gain lossy compression, but at the default 92% quality the result is visually identical for screen use and normal print sizes. Serious editors keep the RAW originals; everyone converts copies for sharing.
Is my work safe here?
Yes — this is a client-side converter. The RAW decoding engine is downloaded to your browser as WebAssembly and runs on your CPU. Your files are never transmitted, stored or seen by any server. You can even disconnect from the internet after the page loads and it will still work.
Is WebP safe to use everywhere now?
On the web, yes — Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all support it natively, and it now accounts for a large share of served images. Desktop software is the weak spot: older Photoshop, Office and viewer versions may not open it. For web use, choose WebP; for files that must open anywhere, choose JPG.
Is there a file size or batch limit?
No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
