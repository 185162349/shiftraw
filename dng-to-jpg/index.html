Why convert DNG to JPG?

DNG (Digital Negative) is Adobe's open RAW format, published in 2004 as a standardized alternative to the zoo of proprietary camera formats. Some cameras write DNG natively — Leica, Pentax, Ricoh, DJI drones, most Blackmagic cinema cameras — and Lightroom can convert any proprietary RAW into DNG for archival.

Being open does not make DNG files any easier to share: they are still raw sensor data, still large, and still ignored by anything that isn't photo software. If you archived your library as DNG and now need web-ready images, a converter is the bridge.

ShiftRaw converts DNG to JPG in your browser with no upload. The LibRaw engine handles both linear and mosaic DNG variants, and your files never leave your device.

Best for: JPG is the universal choice: every device, browser, app and platform displays it, and at 90%+ quality the compression is invisible. A 45-megapixel RAW frame converts to a 5–10 MB JPG that uploads anywhere. For web and client delivery it remains the sensible default — start here unless you specifically need transparency (PNG) or the smallest possible files (WebP).

Frequently asked questions

Is DNG better than proprietary RAW formats like CR2 or NEF?
DNG is better for archiving and portability: the specification is open, so software will likely be able to read it decades from now. Proprietary formats sometimes carry slightly more camera-specific data. Many photographers shoot proprietary RAW and archive to DNG — ShiftRaw converts either kind.
Will converting DNG to JPG preserve my Lightroom edits?
Only if you exported the DNG from Lightroom with edits baked in, or exported a linear DNG. A plain raw DNG carries the original sensor data; ShiftRaw renders it with the embedded as-shot white balance.
Why is my DNG file so big?
If the DNG is linear (fully demosaiced) or contains the original RAW embedded — an option in Lightroom's conversion settings — the file can be larger than the source RAW. For delivery purposes, converting to JPG typically reduces file size by 85–95%.
Can I convert DNG back to CR2 or NEF?
No — proprietary camera formats are not openly writable, and DNG conversion is one-way. That's exactly why DNG exists as the archival endpoint, and why converting DNG to universally readable formats like JPG or PNG is the practical path for sharing.
JPG or PNG — which should I choose?
JPG for photos: smaller files, universally supported, visually lossless at high quality. PNG for graphics that need transparency, screenshots, or when you must not lose a single pixel. For photographic work, JPG at 92% is the right call 95% of the time.
Is there a file size or batch limit?
No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
