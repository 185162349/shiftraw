Why convert NEF to

NEF (Nikon Electronic Format) is Nikon's RAW format, and one of the longest-lived in the industry — Nikon has been writing NEF files since the D1 in 1999, and the format lives on today in the Z-series mirrorless bodies. A NEF file stores 12- or 14-bit sensor readings along with Nikon's metadata for white balance, Picture Control and lens data.

Nikon shooters accumulate NEFs by the thousand because the format is excellent for editing, but painful to share: stock Windows installations (outside Nikon's own NX Studio), most content platforms and every messaging app need a JPEG or PNG instead.

ShiftRaw converts NEF to WebP directly in the browser. The LibRaw engine applies your camera's recorded white balance, processes the full-resolution frame, and outputs a clean file — your photos are never uploaded to any server.

Best for: WebP is the modern option: 25–35% smaller than JPG at the same visual quality, with alpha-channel support, and every current browser displays it. If your images are headed for the web — a site, blog, or web app — WebP is the best-performing choice and has been natively supported across all major browsers since 2020.

Frequently asked questions

What does NEF stand for?
Nikon Electronic Format. It is Nikon's proprietary RAW image format, storing unprocessed sensor data plus shooting metadata such as white balance, Picture Control settings and lens information.
Do NEF files from the Z-series work here?
Yes. NEF files from the Z5, Z6, Z7, Z6II, Z7II, Z8 and Z9 all decode through the same LibRaw engine used by this converter, along with every Nikon DSLR NEF back to the D1 era.
What's the best quality setting for NEF to JPG?
For sharing and web use, the default 92% JPG quality is visually indistinguishable from maximum at roughly half the file size. Choose 90%+ for client delivery, or switch to PNG when you need a lossless, uncompressed copy.
Does converting NEF lose the edits from Capture NX or Lightroom?
Yes — those edits live in sidecar files or catalogs, not inside the NEF itself. ShiftRaw renders the original raw data with the camera's as-shot white balance. Finish your raw development first if you need developed results, or use ShiftRaw when you need quick, straight conversions of unedited files.
Is WebP safe to use everywhere now?
On the web, yes — Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge all support it natively, and it now accounts for a large share of served images. Desktop software is the weak spot: older Photoshop, Office and viewer versions may not open it. For web use, choose WebP; for files that must open anywhere, choose JPG.
Is there a file size or batch limit?
No hard limits. Files are processed one by one entirely on your device, so the practical ceiling is your machine's memory - a 100 MP medium-format RAW decodes comfortably on any modern computer. Large batches simply queue and run sequentially.
